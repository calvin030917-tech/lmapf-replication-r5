TITLE|Diagnosing Single-Robot Priority Promotion in Lifelong Warehouse Path Finding
SUB|Fixed-Reservation Checks and Validation on Public Warehouse Benchmarks
ABSTRACT|In warehouse planning with limited search resources per robot, moving one robot to the front of the priority order can change its access to search, its realized service, and the delays of other robots. We intervene once at the same snapshot and budget. Step-by-step replay and an independent time-layer reference establish the limits of judging service timeliness from ranks and planned actions. A separately implemented rolling safe-interval planner generates 128 source runs with sustained service and 460 paired snapshots on two Moving AI warehouse maps. Of 39 earlier focal completions, first-step direction and two-step remaining distance identify 1 and 22, respectively. In 30 cases, another robot finishes later. Across 920 focal queries, 3,680 time-layer checks verify the labels for budget, reservations, goal occupancy and window length. Earlier transfer tests on synthetic layouts included 532 pairs with recursive seeds and 720 with serial seeds under heavy stagnation. Goal-rule replay in the original planner, continuing-policy comparisons in new scenarios, and analyses of aligned tasks establish the scope of the diagnosis.
KEY|lifelong multi-agent path finding, prioritized planning, paired replanning, service timeliness, search budget
H1|1 Introduction
P|A warehouse robot receives its next goal as soon as it reaches its current one. Classical multi-agent path finding (MAPF) starts with fixed start-goal pairs. Its lifelong variant (LMAPF) considers sustained operation with a stream of goals [1–3]. Here, a navigation assignment counts as one task, timed from goal issuance. Online multi-agent pickup and delivery (MAPD) also includes order release, assignment, pickup, and delivery [2], so its service time covers a fuller workflow.
P|Rolling-Horizon Collision Resolution (RHCR), by Li et al., uses continuous cross-traffic to illustrate how high throughput can coexist with individual robots remaining blocked. It monitors progress through goal visits or remaining cost [3]. Asano et al. measure delay against an unimpeded trajectory and use counterfactual actions to distinguish when to advance or yield [4]. Zheng et al.'s RL-RH-PP samples fleet-wide priority orders from the same state, compares shortest-path headings with planned moves, and studies congestion recovery after a policy switch [5, §6]. For fixed goals with a total priority order, Morag et al. distinguish failure of greedy sequential planning from infeasibility under the priority constraints [13].
P|We move one focal robot to the front once and preserve every other relative rank. The estimand is the effect of this change on realized actions and current tasks, conditional on the simulation state, initial reservations, node cap, and follow-on policy. We record search initiation, the first two executed moves, exit from the bottleneck strip, and completion for each robot. This tests whether gaining search changes the actual moves, whether changing moves speeds up service, and whether a focal gain coincides with delays for others. Local controls on fixed reservations and goal occupancy provide finer explanations. We compare what ranks and planned headings reveal on the same snapshots and verify the service clocks through independent replay. A separate planner capable of sustained operation, full public warehouse maps, and an explicit time-layer reference test these local judgments.

H2|2.2 Planning Mechanisms and Scope of Evidence
P|Sequential planners allocate search along the current priority order. PBS branches over partial orders in response to conflicts [7]. PIBT inherits priorities and backtracks when making each step. Its arrival guarantee depends on conditions such as edges belonging to suitable cycles [8]. Roadmap methods with safe locations rely on available retreat space and their conflict-handling rules [9]. Given higher-priority reservations, SIPP compresses the feasible times for an individual robot into safe intervals [6]. Local order, retreat space, and the semantics of reservations can each change the feasible set.
P|WinC-MAPF achieves completeness through joint heuristic updates, independent grouping, and a suitable action generator [10]. DAG-ECBS extends the approach to group solvers that satisfy bounded-suboptimality conditions [11]. Real-Time LaCAM retains incremental joint search to meet its completeness conditions [12]. These guarantees identify when a short window can sustain progress. Our greedy per-robot replacements only require a conflict-free current window for commitment, so we track realized execution across rounds. Traffic guidance directly changes path costs and flow across aisles [14–16]. Fairness in travel delay, fairness of fixed-goal paths, and order timeliness measure service at different levels [4,17,18].
H1|3 Model, Metrics, and Single-Round Procedure
H2|3.1 Task Age and Two Service Clocks
P|A robot receives a goal at time a, at an initial static shortest-path distance d. At the end of step t, a task is overdue when its relative age (t−a)/d strictly exceeds τ. The completion step is assigned to the old task before the next one is issued. Consecutive identical goals are excluded. External zero-distance tasks can be recorded as immediate completions. Let Dᵢ be the overdue steps of robot i among N robots observed over T steps. Equation (1) gives the fleet-wide share. The main threshold is τ=3, with sensitivity at 2 and 4. We report the median, quartiles, and maximum of per-robot shares, and the longest continuous overdue spell. A new task ends a spell. An ongoing spell at the end of measurement is right-censored. P|A range of map columns defines the bottleneck strip. A robot enters the exit analysis if its current task starts and ends on opposite sides and it has yet to reach the goal side at the snapshot. F is the time to first entry, with F=0 for a robot already inside at step 0. E is the first exit to the goal side, occurring before or at completion of the current task. L is the last entry before E and is 0 when the robot starts inside and never retreats. The components F, L−F, and E−L measure time before first entry, time between first and last entry, and the final crossing. We count failures to enter separately from entries without exit. If a robot retreats, L−F includes earlier attempts and requeueing. E−L measures the last successful crossing.
P|The current task completes on the first visit to its goal after the snapshot. The follow-up cap is H=32. A missing completion or exit is assigned time 32, with a separate occurrence indicator. Completion at exactly step 32 counts as observed and ties an unfinished task on the capped clock. Differences are promotion minus original order, so a negative value indicates an earlier event. We retain occurrence indicators and event times per robot in each branch, and account for within-scenario dependence in robot-level summaries.

H2|3.3 Rolling Planning and Compute Accounting
P|The main synthetic-layout experiment uses a planning window of W=8 and an execution prefix of 2 moves. The recursive seed orders robots by issue time and ID, assigning candidates at most one step away to build each new layer. It tries adjacent cells and waiting in order of static remaining distance. Moving takes precedence over waiting on ties, followed by cell ID. If an unclaimed destination is occupied by an unassigned robot, the procedure recursively assigns that robot's next cell. It rejects shared cells and swaps, and withdraws a failed claim before trying the next action. The selected positions form the next layer, and the process repeats 8 times. Seed construction uses only issued goals. The per-robot replacement pass then uses the tested order.
ALGO
P|The low-level space-time weighted A* uses the key (t+2dₜ, dₜ, insertion order), where dₜ is the static remaining distance at the current cell. It enumerates adjacent cells in row-major order, followed by waiting, and enqueues each (cell, t) once. A goal candidate must be able to stay at its goal through the window. A path reaching the window boundary can also be submitted. A queue pop tests termination before checking the remaining budget. Once the last expansion has been charged, a subsequent terminal pop can still yield an accepted path. A query with no initial budget does not start. The budget counts expanded nonterminal space-time states. Seed construction, sorting, reservation updates, and safety checks add separate per-round work.
P|At each step, the robots execute the joint prefix already committed. A goal reached on step 1 is registered immediately and triggers the next goal. Step 2 still executes the committed move, and reaching the newly issued goal on that step also counts as a completion. Replanning then uses the currently issued goals. The simulator generates the task streams in advance, and each planning query sees only the goal issued at that time. The full replays include the positions, task indices, reservations of all other robots and remaining budget before each first-round query, returned candidates, and stepwise trajectories in both branches.
H2|3.4 Reference Models and Mechanism Controls
P|For frozen tasks, joint breadth-first search (BFS) enumerates combinations of waiting and adjacent moves, excluding shared cells and swaps. Goal holding locks a robot at its first arrival. Goal release records that arrival and allows it to depart. We declare infeasibility under a given constraint model only after exhausting the reachable joint states, and classify search cutoffs as unknown. Online runs also reissue goals and execute rolling prefixes, so the frozen reference applies to the stated tasks, goal semantics, and committed actions.
P|The mechanism checks use sampled overdue snapshots. Within each map, fleet size, budget, and source grouping, we sort by seed and then snapshot time. For the first 3 original-order focal queries that were skipped or cut off, we hold all other reservations at query time fixed and add 32 nodes. For the first 3 pairs per stratum, we also check sequentially whether the first higher-priority candidate can be replaced. We reverse neighbor enumeration while preserving total work and arrival time or window-end distance, recheck the executed prefix for the round, and run another local query with a uniform 32-node cap. In case B, a finite time-layer search over the entire window separately compares goal holding and release. The reference minimizes first entry to the goal cell subject to a complete collision-free suffix.

P|The independent implementation uses rolling Safe Interval Path Planning (SIPP) [6]. From the issued goals it recursively builds a 10-step safe seed, with rotating tie-breaks among robots of equal age. Each query first removes that robot's own reservations. It stores the earliest arrival in each unoccupied interval of a cell, uses static remaining distance as a heuristic, and checks swaps at transitions. It can select a legal candidate that holds its goal or submit a safe path through the window. Each round shares a cap of 6N or 18N interval-state expansions and executes 2 moves. The seed constructor, reservation table, low-level search, and task advancement were implemented separately. The two planners count interval and space-time states, respectively.
P|The source order uses local congestion. We inspect one snapshot every 8 steps from 56 through 200. The focal robot has the largest relative age among robots that do not rank first, received their task at least 2 steps ago, did not reduce remaining distance on the preceding move, and are at static distance at most 24. We then take the first 6 moves along a deterministic static shortest path without reservations. The shared-edge label applies if another robot intends to traverse the same undirected edge in the opposite direction with an estimated entry time at most 2 steps apart. Per trajectory we randomly draw up to 2 snapshots per label. To estimate the pooled difference in proportions, we resample seeds within map and fleet size 10,000 times, keeping budgets and snapshots together in each scenario. We report a pooled interval when each group has at least 12 independent scenarios, and retain the actual denominators for sparse strata.
P|We make two comparisons on identical snapshots. Following Zheng et al.'s same-state sampling and direction analysis [5, §6], each state is evaluated under the original order, focal promotion, and 6 fixed random permutations, with the same planner, seed window, and budget. A first move toward the goal scores +1, waiting 0, and moving away −1. The first criterion predicts earlier completion from a higher post-promotion direction score. The second uses shorter remaining distance after 2 moves. Both are untrained rules using observables available during planning. The service check examines every cell in all 32-step trajectories, including conflicts and goal visits, and compares the computed arrivals with the respective execution records.
P|We label local mechanisms using four controls. B means the actual query was skipped or cut off, and an increased cap yields a goal-holding arrival under the same reservations and window. R means no legal arrival with a continuation to the window end exists under fixed reservations, while removing other robots' reservations yields an arrival in the same window. G means allowing departure after arrival yields an earlier feasible arrival or makes an infeasible holding query feasible. W means the original window has no solution even with goal release, while extending the same reservation prefix by holding the other robots' final cells for 8 more steps yields a feasible arrival. We query each setting separately with intervals and verify the earliest arrivals and witnesses by explicit time-layer enumeration of cell, time, and arrival information. Seven small cases with predetermined labels cover individual, combined, and absent mechanisms. Local labels can overlap and apply to the stated counterfactual inputs.
P|We use new seeds 7101–7116 to compare two-stage ordering with longest remaining distance under the same SIPP implementation. The continuing-task metrics describe the tasks actually received and processed under each policy. The aligned cohort separately follows each robot's first task, issued at step 0 in both arms, matching robot ID, task index, and issue time for 64 steps. Throughput, overdue burden, and initial-task effects are paired by scenario within stratum, with two-sided pointwise 95% intervals.

H1|6 Discussion
P|On matched snapshots, the two planning observables miss 38 and 17 replay-verified earlier completions, respectively. Even with matching ranks and first 2 moves, local searches can face different feasibility constraints. The independent time-layer reference makes these causes testable through fixed-input counterfactuals that increase the budget, remove reservations, change goal occupancy, or extend the window. The labels can coexist. A robot can lose a short-window opportunity due to reservations and regain feasibility in an extended window with fixed reservations. The results identify conditions that could be adjusted. Removing reservations or changing occupancy at the window boundary is a local query. Other robots' closed-loop responses require executed replay.
P|The case B rerun shows that changing the first focal query's goal-holding condition alters service under the same promoted order and compute cap. This case and the controlled examples provide mechanism witnesses. Operational interventions can affect later reservations, budgets, and goal issuance, so outcome sizes remain state- and implementation-dependent. The shared-edge association occurs mainly on W2, suggesting a role for alternative routes and traffic load alongside the resource definition.
P|The experiments cover four synthetic and two public warehouse grids, with up to 48 robots. New runs use generated demand, static four-neighbor maps, and service follow-up of at most 32 steps. The estimand concerns paired snapshots under the fixed inclusion rules. Both planners use prioritization and safe reservations. Other planning families and real order systems require their own validation. Policy results apply to the tested rules and budgets. Per-round timing describes the recorded single-machine environment.
H1|7 Conclusion
P|Matched-state promotion and replay show whether search, execution, and service across robots change together. On public layouts with an independent planner that sustains service, replay identifies earlier completions and delays to other robots that planned directions miss. The time-layer reference checks four local mechanisms. Together, these results identify the information that the diagnostic adds. The historical joint decision criteria and the new policy runs provide no evidence of a stable service advantage for the two-stage rule.
